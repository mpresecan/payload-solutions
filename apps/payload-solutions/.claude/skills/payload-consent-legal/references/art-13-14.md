# The privacy policy: Articles 13 and 14

Article 13 covers data collected from the person. Article 14 covers data obtained about them
from somewhere else — an enrichment provider, a partner, a public register. Most projects need
Art. 13 and one paragraph of Art. 14; if any data arrives from a third party, Art. 14(2)(f)
requires you to name the source.

Article 12(1) sits over both: concise, transparent, intelligible, plain language. It is a
requirement, not a style note, and a policy that satisfies every item below in impenetrable
prose still fails it.

## The mandatory items

**Identity and contact details of the controller — 13(1)(a).** The legal entity, plus contact
details. A postal address is expected; a support email alone has repeatedly been held not to be
enough. If there is an EU representative under Art. 27, they are named here too.

**The DPO's contact details — 13(1)(b).** Only if one is appointed. Where a DPO exists and is
not published, the disclosure is simply missing.

**Purposes and legal basis — 13(1)(c).** Per purpose, not per document. "We process your data
to provide and improve our services, on the basis of our legitimate interests and your consent"
covers nothing: which purpose rests on which basis?

**The legitimate interests relied on — 13(1)(d).** Where Art. 6(1)(f) is a basis, the specific
interest must be stated. "Our legitimate business interests" is circular. "Preventing fraudulent
chargebacks" is an interest.

**Recipients or categories of recipients — 13(1)(e).** Who receives the data. Naming them is
better than categories, and a generated table from the processor register is better than both,
because it cannot drift. Look for a `{{processor-table:recipients}}` block.

**Transfers and safeguards — 13(1)(f).** Where data goes outside the EEA: the mechanism, and how
to obtain a copy of the safeguards. See `transfers.md`.

**Retention — 13(2)(a).** A period, or the criteria used to determine one. "As long as
necessary" alone does not satisfy this; "as long as the account is open, then 90 days" does.

**Rights — 13(2)(b).** Access, rectification, erasure, restriction, objection, portability. All
six, plus a route to use them. A list of rights with no address is a list.

**Withdrawal of consent — 13(2)(c).** Where consent is a basis: withdrawable at any time,
without affecting the lawfulness of what happened before. Only relevant where consent is
actually relied on — do not add it to a policy that relies on contract throughout.

**Right to complain — 13(2)(d).** To a supervisory authority. Naming the relevant one is what
makes the right usable.

**Whether providing data is required — 13(2)(e).** Statutory or contractual requirement, whether
they are obliged to provide it, and the consequences of not doing so.

**Automated decision-making — 13(2)(f).** If it happens, meaningful information about the logic
and the consequences. If it does not, the policy should not hedge as though it might.

## Common failures worth raising

- **Consent claimed where consent is not used.** A policy that says "by using our site you
  consent" while relying on contract and legitimate interests describes a different legal
  posture than the one the system implements.
- **The cookie banner and the policy disagree** about what runs before a decision.
- **Recipients written out in prose** and last updated two vendors ago.
- **Retention stated once, globally**, for a system that obviously keeps invoices longer than
  session logs.
- **"We may share your data with third parties"** — a sentence that discloses nothing.
- **Rights listed without the withdrawal right**, in a policy that relies on consent.
- **A US-style "we do not sell your data"** dropped into an EEA policy where the question is not
  whether you sell it but what your basis is.

## What good looks like

Purposes as a table or a list with a basis and a retention period against each. Recipients as a
generated table. Transfers with the mechanism named. Rights with an address next to them. The
whole thing readable in ten minutes by someone who does not work there.
