Most projects already keep the people they want to write to in Payload — users, customers, a newsletter sign-up collection. Resend Broadcasts turns those collections into Resend contacts and segments, and gives your team a place in the admin to write a campaign, see it exactly as one particular subscriber will, send themselves a test, and send or schedule it through [Resend Broadcasts](https://resend.com/docs/dashboard/broadcasts/introduction).

```ts title="payload.config.ts"
resendBroadcastsPlugin({
  from: 'Acme <news@acme.com>',
  sources: [
    { collection: 'users' },
    { collection: 'subscribers', defaultSubscribed: true },
  ],
})
```

That is the whole setup. Every save of a `users` or `subscribers` document keeps its Resend contact current. An editor creates a **List** — "Everyone on the Pro plan" — which becomes a Resend segment, writes a **Campaign** in Lexical with `{{firstName|there}}` in it, opens **Preview & send**, clicks through the people in the list to check the copy, and sends.

## What each side owns

| Payload owns | Resend owns |
| --- | --- |
| who your subscribers are, their names and properties | delivery, at scale, from your verified domain |
| which documents belong to which list | the per-contact unsubscribe link and preference page |
| the campaign copy, its preview and its test sends | filling each contact's variables into the broadcast |
| the design — a React Email template in your repo | skipping everyone who unsubscribed |

Payload is the source of truth for **who exists and who is in which list**. Resend is the source of truth for **unsubscribes**: when a reader unsubscribes through Resend's link, a webhook switches their document off in Payload, and a resync never subscribes them again.

## How it fits together

| Piece | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `resendBroadcastsPlugin()` | adds the fields, hooks, collections, global, endpoints and admin views |
| a **source** | a collection whose documents are subscribers; `email`, `name` and `subscribed` are added if missing |
| **Lists** (`newsletter-lists`) | one Resend segment each: a source, optionally narrowed by a Payload query |
| **Campaigns** (`newsletter-campaigns`) | subject, preview text and a Lexical body, sent to one list |
| **Preview & send** | the campaign's tab: pick a list and any person in it, test, send or schedule |
| **Newsletter Settings** | sender, reply-to, site name, the footer, the default test recipient |
| **Sync runs** | a record of every reconciliation with Resend |
| `payload.resendBroadcasts` | the same operations from your own code |

## What you get

- Contacts kept in step on every save, email change and delete — names split for Resend, custom properties pushed with them.
- Lists as Resend segments, filled in the background when created or refiltered, and kept current as documents change.
- A preview that renders the campaign for any real subscriber, with a searchable picker and next/previous stepping through the list.
- Variables with fallbacks — `{{firstName|there}}` — that become Resend's own `{{{contact.first_name|there}}}` placeholders at send time.
- An **Image** block for uploads from your media collection, sized for email, with absolute URLs, and a check that refuses to send images readers could not load.
- Test sends through Resend's email API, filled in as the subscriber you picked.
- Send now or schedule, cancel a scheduled send, and pick up the delivered status.
- Unsubscribes synced back by a signed webhook, and a full resync for existing data or drift.
- An in-memory Resend mock for development and tests, so nothing needs a Resend account until you point it at one.

## What it is not

It is not an email builder: the design is a React Email component your developers own, and the body is written in a deliberately small Lexical editor. It does not do transactional email — that is what [Payload Emails](/docs/plugins/payload-emails) is for, and the two share the same template contract. It does not report opens and clicks inside Payload yet; Resend's dashboard has them for every broadcast.
